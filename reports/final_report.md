# Final report: EEG representations and sentence information in ZuCo

## Question
Can EEG recorded while people read sentences identify the sentence they read, and does this hold for unseen subjects, noisy signals, and a second dataset?

## Data
- **ZuCo 1.0:** 12 subjects, natural reading, 105 EEG channels at 500 Hz. 71 preprocessed block recordings (subject ZPH is missing block 2). The "Matlab files" give 300 sentences per subject with the true sentence, raw per-sentence EEG, the authors' band features, and per-word features.
- **ZuCo 2.0:** 8 of 18 subjects (56 block recordings, same 105 channels and 500 Hz).
- The channel labels and their order are identical in both datasets; the reference channel Cz is all zeros and was dropped (104 channels).
- Recordings were already preprocessed by the dataset authors (Automagic: high-pass 0.5 Hz, 50 Hz notch/low-pass); we did not re-filter.

## Methods
- **Units:** sentence windows from triggers 10 -> 11 (ZuCo 1.0: 3,125 windows; ZuCo 2.0: 2,424), aligned to sentences by order and duration (this alignment is inferred, not given), and true-label sentence rows from the Matlab files (12 subjects, 3,323 usable sentence rows).
- **Targets:** sentence embeddings from a MiniLM sentence-transformer, L2-normalised.
- **Decoder (identical for every representation):** standardisation fitted on training rows, ridge regression into the embedding space (strength chosen on validation subjects), cosine ranking over the unique test sentences.
- **Representations (three approaches):**
  1. Handcrafted: band-power features (whole sentence and 1.5 s chunks) and the authors' band features.
  2. Self-supervised on ZuCo: a small contrastive 1D-CNN trained without labels on training subjects' training-sentence chunks only (8 epochs; two augmented views per chunk; noise, amplitude scaling, small shifts). An identical untrained encoder is the control.
  3. Frozen pretrained: CBraMod (ICLR 2025, Hugging Face `braindecode/cbramod-pretrained`), applied to the 104 channels at 200 Hz in 1 s patches (4.9M parameters), features extracted without any training by us. The same network with random initialisation is the control. CBraMod was pretrained on 19-channel 10-20 recordings, so the 104-channel EGI montage is outside its training distribution.
- **Controls:** random input through the same pipeline; a reading-length/duration-only baseline (no EEG); and, at word level, a behaviour-only model (first-fixation time + word length, no EEG).
- **Splits:** leave-subjects-out folds with 45 held-out test sentences and 45 validation sentences, so test subjects and test sentences are never in training; a leakage check runs on every fold. Identical sentences share one group.
- **Noise and quality:** white noise at 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 times each channel's standard deviation added to **test** EEG only; quality weighting down-weights training rows with a high artifact score.
- **Transfer:** a decoder trained on one dataset only, evaluated on the other (no target labels).
- **Word-level GRU:** a small GRU reads the words of a sentence in order and is trained to match the sentence embedding. Input per word: random numbers, behaviour only, EEG (train-statistics or per-subject normalisation), or EEG + behaviour. 12 subjects, 4 folds, 45 candidates.
- **Earlier runs (continuous windows):** CBraMod noise and quality weighting, a self-supervised encoder with noise, and encoder transfer between datasets were run on the continuous windows (inferred alignment, 4 folds) by `run_pretrained.py` and `run_transfer_encoders.py`. These scripts depend on a removed script and cannot be rerun; their result CSVs are kept. Features: CBraMod encoder output averaged over patches, then mean and SD across channels.
- **Metrics:** top-1, recall@K, MRR, median rank, `sim_margin`, with chance levels. For the GRU comparison, 95% bootstrap confidence intervals over test subjects.
- **Decision rule (fixed before the GRU and CBraMod results):** an EEG model counts as a real finding only if its paired difference (bootstrap over test subjects) against both random input and the behaviour-only control is entirely above 0. Per-subject normalisation is transductive and always reported separately.

## Results
- **Sentence retrieval is at chance for every EEG representation.** In the main true-label experiment (45 candidates, 4 folds) EEG methods reach top-1 of 1.8-3.3% against 2.2% chance and 2.2% for random input; `sim_margin` is 0.0005-0.0086 (SD over folds 0.003-0.009) against 0.0000 for random input. The reading-length control alone gets 0.0093.
- **Continuous-EEG windows, unseen subjects and sentences:** ZuCo 1.0 top-1 2.8% (random 2.5%, chance 2.6%); ZuCo 2.0 2.0% (random 1.8%, chance 2.3%). Without holding out sentences (about 260-300 candidates) EEG is also at chance. A small ZuCo 1.0 effect after per-subject standardisation (`sim_margin` 0.0138, SD 0.0088 over 4 folds) did not appear in ZuCo 2.0 (-0.0020), and the length-only control is of similar size in the easier setting.
- **Self-supervised learning did not help.** The contrastive loss fell from about 3.5 to 1.9 over 8 epochs, so the encoder learned structure in the EEG, but its sentence retrieval (`sim_margin` 0.0005) equals the untrained encoder (0.0000). In the earlier continuous-window run the trained encoder was also not better than the untrained one (clean `sim_margin`: ZuCo 1.0 0.0035 vs 0.0056; ZuCo 2.0 -0.0045 vs -0.0034).
- **Frozen pretrained CBraMod did not help.** On its own row set (3,266 sentence rows, 45 candidates, chance top-1 2.22%, chance MRR 0.0977), CBraMod pretrained reaches top-1 2.07%, MRR 0.1026, `sim_margin` 0.0023 (SD 0.0034 over folds). Random input gets 1.94%, 0.0981 and -0.0010; our band power 2.28%, 0.1020 and 0.0052; the no-EEG reading-length control 2.27%, 0.1033 and 0.0086; the same network with random initialisation 1.85%, 0.0937 and -0.0010. With per-subject normalisation (transductive) CBraMod pretrained gets `sim_margin` 0.0028. The differences between methods are as small as the spread across folds, so none counts as a finding.
- **CBraMod under noise and with quality weighting (earlier continuous-window run, 4 folds, inferred alignment).** Frozen CBraMod `sim_margin` from clean to strong noise: ZuCo 1.0 0.0033, 0.0025, 0.0015, -0.0001; ZuCo 2.0 0.0061, 0.0089, 0.0047, 0.0015. It drifts toward 0 at strong noise, but the clean values are already within the fold-to-fold spread of 0 (SD 0.0068 and 0.0038), so no real information is shown to be lost. With quality weights: ZuCo 1.0 0.0033 to -0.0006, ZuCo 2.0 -0.0037 to -0.0012, i.e. no benefit. Random input gets -0.0012 (ZuCo 1.0) and -0.0023 (ZuCo 2.0); untrained CBraMod -0.0019 and 0.0059. In ZuCo 2.0 frozen CBraMod is above random input, but the untrained network matches it, so pretraining does not explain it; this is exploratory and not a finding. This file has no reading-length control row.
- **Noise (true-label experiment):** performance does not change with noise because there is no sentence information to lose; the curves are flat at chance. This was tested for band power and the self-supervised encoder in the true-label experiment, and for CBraMod in the earlier run above.
- **Quality weighting:** no reliable difference (band-power chunks 0.0086 with weights vs 0.0071 without; encoder -0.0003 vs 0.0005; spread across folds about 0.005-0.009).
- **Unseen subjects:** the same chance-level result holds for held-out subjects in every fold; the spread across folds is reported above.
- **Cross-dataset transfer:** at chance in both directions (ZuCo 1.0 -> 2.0 top-1 0.48% vs 0.34% random; ZuCo 2.0 -> 1.0 0.53% vs 0.42% random). In the separate earlier run with encoders (255 and 226 candidates, source-only decoder, unseen target sentences), frozen CBraMod gets top-1 0.17% (ZuCo 1.0 -> 2.0, chance 0.39%) and 0.12% (ZuCo 2.0 -> 1.0, chance 0.44%), the self-supervised encoder 0.39% and 0.32%, and random input 0.42% and 0.37%; `sim_margin` is between -0.0031 and 0.0032 for all methods. Band power from a single run is slightly above chance for ZuCo 2.0 -> 1.0 (0.63% vs 0.44%) but not for ZuCo 1.0 -> 2.0 (0.35% vs 0.39%), so it is not counted. Frozen CBraMod is not better than its untrained version in either direction.
- **Word level (48,638 word rows):** word retrieval equals the random-input control (top-1 3.9% vs 3.7%; reading-length control 4.8%), and sentence retrieval from word-level EEG is at chance (MRR 0.094 vs 0.095 random vs 0.098 chance).
- **Word-level GRU and the behaviour control (12 subjects, MRR above chance, 95% CI over subjects):** random input 0.0015 [-0.0080, 0.0114]; behaviour only (no EEG) 0.0803 [0.0623, 0.0964]; EEG with train-statistics normalisation 0.0035 [-0.0057, 0.0155]; EEG with per-subject normalisation 0.0174 [-0.0061, 0.0425]; EEG + behaviour 0.0260 [0.0091, 0.0441] with per-subject normalisation and -0.0014 [-0.0088, 0.0059] with train-statistics normalisation. Paired MRR differences: honest EEG minus random +0.0020 [-0.0121, 0.0183]; per-subject-normalised EEG minus behaviour-only -0.0629 [-0.0925, -0.0296]; per-subject-normalised EEG + behaviour minus behaviour-only -0.0543 [-0.0734, -0.0338]. Per-subject-normalised EEG is above random input on `sim_margin` (+0.0124 [0.0031, 0.0225]) but not on MRR, and it is clearly below the behaviour-only model. By the decision rule no EEG model is a real finding.
- **Positive control:** the EEG features predict log first-fixation duration within a subject (r = 0.146, SD 0.072 over 12 subjects, random 0.012) and across subjects after per-subject standardisation (r = 0.082, random -0.007), but not word length (r about 0.008). So the pipeline can detect real information; what is present in these features is reading-related (eye-movement/reading-time), not sentence content.

## Did pretrained representations help?
No. A frozen pretrained EEG encoder (CBraMod) gave sentence retrieval at chance, no better than band power or the no-EEG reading-length control, and not clearly different from the same network with random initialisation. This held on clean EEG, under added noise, and in transfer between the two datasets. Possible reasons, not tested: the montage mismatch (104 EGI channels vs 19 channels in pretraining), no fine-tuning, or no sentence information in the signal at this sample size. We cannot tell these apart.

## Conclusion
Within the limits below, the answer is no: sentence-level and first-fixation band-power EEG features, a frozen pretrained encoder, a small self-supervised encoder and a word-level GRU do not retrieve the correct sentence beyond random input and reading-behaviour controls, for unseen subjects and across datasets. The detectable EEG information is related to reading behaviour. A word-level model that sees only reading behaviour (no EEG) already beats random input, and EEG adds nothing to it. So good-looking word-level "EEG decoding" numbers need a behaviour-only control before they can be credited to the brain signal. This agrees with the concern in the literature that EEG-to-text results often do not exceed noise-input baselines.

## Limitations
- Only sentence-level and first-fixation band features were tested; time-locked word-level responses (for example N400-type effects) are the main untested direction. A negative result here does not show that no language information exists in EEG.
- 4 folds and one seed. Bootstrap confidence intervals over subjects exist only for the GRU comparison; no significance tests and no multiple-comparison correction; small differences are within fold-to-fold variation.
- Per-subject standardisation is transductive and reported separately.
- The continuous-EEG sentence alignment is inferred; ZuCo 1.0 windows covered 264 of 300 sentences (cause not investigated); ZuCo 2.0 uses 8 of 18 subjects, and its Matlab files (34.6 GB) were not downloaded.
- Noise is synthetic and the quality score is a simple artifact fraction. In the true-label experiment CBraMod was evaluated on clean EEG only and not fine-tuned; its noise and transfer results come from the earlier continuous-window run, which has inferred alignment and no reading-length control row.
- The earlier-run scripts (`run_pretrained.py`, `run_transfer_encoders.py`) cannot be rerun because they import a removed script; their results are kept as saved CSVs and are not part of the generated `RESULTS.md`.
- The self-supervised encoder is small, trained for 8 epochs, and untuned.
- The CBraMod true-label evaluation uses its own row set (3,266 rows), so its control numbers differ slightly from the main table.
- Word-retrieval chance is not meaningful because frequent words dominate; use the random-input control.
